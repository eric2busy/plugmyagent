Set up your agent:
one page, every agent.

Pick your tab, follow the steps, end with a working agent. Same playbook for all nine: account, app, first tool, first test. Then connect your first API.

Muse

10 minutesBeginnerFree to start
What you end up with

A Muse agent on your phone, ready in 10 minutes. This is the fastest path, no terminal, hosted for you. Want it on your own machine instead? That is the OpenClaw tab.

01Create your account

Open muse.ai in your browser and sign up. Use an email address you actually check: login codes and important notices go there.

Referral tip

New to Muse? Enter the code DGC4EX in Settings within 48 hours of joining: you get 1 billion tokens, and so does the person who invited you. Disclosure: this is our referral code. It costs you nothing, and we both earn tokens if you use it.

What success looks like

You are logged in at muse.ai and you see the chat window. That is your agent's home base.

02Get the app on your phone

Search for Muse in the App Store (iPhone) or Google Play (Android) and install the official app. Log in with the same account you just created.

Prefer the browser? That works too. The point is having your agent one tap away instead of buried in a tab you never open.

What success looks like

You are logged in on your phone. Same agent, same memory, two places.

03Connect WhatsApp (optional, recommended)

In Settings, find WhatsApp linking and follow the prompts to connect your number. This puts your agent inside the app you already check fifty times a day.

What success looks like

Send your agent a message from WhatsApp and get a reply. Now it lives where you live.

04Give it one real task

Do not start with "what can you do." Start with something from your actual life.

Copy, paste, customize

"Here is what is on my plate this week: [paste your list]. Turn it into a prioritized plan for Monday to Friday, with time estimates. Flag anything that looks unrealistic."

What success looks like

It hands back a finished plan, not just advice. If it asks a clarifying question first, that is good: it is scoping the job before doing it.

When something goes wrong

I am outside the US or Canada and cannot sign up

Muse currently serves the US and Canada. If you are elsewhere, skip to the OpenClaw tab: OpenClaw is self-hosted and has no region limits.

The WhatsApp code never arrives

Check the number for typos, wait two minutes, then request a new code. Still nothing? Use the app or the web for now and retry tomorrow. This step is optional.

It answered like a chatbot instead of doing the work

Give it the delegation recipe from guide 001: the outcome, the context, the constraints, and the check ("show me before you send anything"). Then run the task again.

OpenClaw

20 minutesIntermediateFree and open source
What you end up with

An OpenClaw agent running on your own machine. This is the self-hosted path, fully yours, terminal required. Want the 10-minute phone path instead? That is the Muse tab.

01Install Node.js 22 or newer

OpenClaw runs on Node.js, version 22 or higher. Check what you have:

node --version

No Node yet, or the version is too old? Install the LTS release from nodejs.org, then close your terminal completely and open a fresh one.

What success looks like

The terminal prints a version like v22.14.0 or higher.

02Install OpenClaw

On macOS or Linux, run the official installer:

macOS / Linux
curl -fsSL https://openclaw.ai/install.sh | bash

On Windows, open PowerShell and run:

Windows PowerShell
iwr -useb https://openclaw.ai/install.ps1 | iex

Prefer npm? This works too:

npm install -g openclaw@latest
What success looks like

openclaw --version prints a version number. The command exists and your terminal knows where it lives.

03Run the onboarding wizard

openclaw onboard --install-daemon

The wizard walks you through three decisions in plain words:

  1. Which AI model powers your agent. Pick Anthropic, OpenAI, OpenRouter, or a local model via Ollama. The hosted options need an API key; the wizard asks for it and stores it securely.
  2. How the gateway runs. Accept the default: the wizard installs it as a background service so your agent keeps working when the terminal is closed.
  3. Which chat channels to connect. WhatsApp, Telegram, Discord. You can add these later too.
What success looks like

The wizard finishes without errors. Your settings live in ~/.openclaw/openclaw.json and your agent's workspace in ~/.openclaw/workspace.

04Check that it is alive

openclaw gateway status
openclaw health
What success looks like

Gateway status says running. Health reports OK. If either one complains, jump to troubleshooting below before continuing.

05Open the dashboard and say hello

openclaw dashboard

Or open http://127.0.0.1:18789/ directly in your browser. This is your agent's control room: chat, integrations, and settings in one place.

Your first message

"Hi. Tell me what you can do, and what you need from me to do it well."

What success looks like

The dashboard loads and your agent answers. You now have a working self-hosted agent.

06Connect your phone (optional)

openclaw channels login

Follow the prompts to link WhatsApp or Telegram. Message your agent from your phone and get a reply.

What success looks like

A message sent from your phone gets an answer from your agent. Same brain, new doorway.

When something goes wrong

openclaw: command not found after installing

Close the terminal completely and open a fresh one so it picks up the new PATH. Still missing? Re-run the installer and read its output for errors.

The gateway will not start, or the port is busy

Run it in the foreground to see the actual error:

openclaw gateway --port 18789

The most common cause is a second copy already running.

On Linux, the agent dies when I log out

The official docs call this out: the background service needs lingering enabled so it survives logout:

sudo loginctl enable-linger $USER
The wizard asks for a model provider and I have no API key

Two free paths: choose the local option via Ollama for a fully free setup, or grab a pay-as-you-go key from OpenRouter. Keys go into the wizard, never into a chat.

ChatGPT

10 minutesBeginnerFree to start
What you end up with

ChatGPT with tools connected, ready to fetch live data. Free tier works for chat; connecting your own tools (MCP) needs a paid plan. Start free, upgrade when you want tools.

01Create your account

Open chatgpt.com in your browser and sign up. Use an email address you actually check: login codes and important notices go there.

What success looks like

You are logged in at chatgpt.com and you see the chat window.

02Get the app

Install the ChatGPT app from the App Store (iPhone) or Google Play (Android) and log in with the same account. The app adds voice, image, and camera input on top of chat.

What success looks like

You are logged in on your phone. Same agent, same history, two places.

03Connect your first tool

Open Settings and look for Apps / Connectors. Start with a prebuilt one: Gmail, Google Drive, or Calendar. Grant access once and ChatGPT can read from it when you ask.

For your own tools (APIs, MCP servers), you need a paid plan: Plus, Pro, or Business. On a paid plan, turn on Developer Mode in Settings, then add a remote MCP server by its URL. The server's tools appear in your chats.

What success looks like

You ask about something only the connected tool knows, and it answers with live data instead of guessing.

04Run the test

Ask one question only the connected tool can answer. Then read guide 004 to connect your first API in 10 minutes: the same three moves (get access, connect it, test it) work here.

What success looks like

A live answer from your tool, not a guess from memory. You have a working agent.

When something goes wrong

I cannot find Developer Mode / MCP

Custom MCP servers need a paid plan (Plus, Pro, or Business) and Developer Mode on. On the free plan, use the prebuilt Apps instead.

It answered from memory instead of using the tool

Name the tool explicitly: "Use the [name] tool and read the number from its response." Then ask again. Guide 004, section 05 covers this fix.

Poke

10 minutesBeginnerNegotiated price
What you end up with

Poke in your messages, at a price you negotiated, with your first integration connected. Poke is the only agent on this page with no published free tier: you haggle once on the monthly price.

01Start Poke

Open poke.com in your browser and follow the prompts to start texting Poke. It runs inside iMessage, SMS, or WhatsApp: the agent lives in your messages, not in a separate app you have to remember to open.

What success looks like

You send Poke a message and get a reply. It lives where you live.

02Set your price

Poke asks you to name your monthly price. Reported prices run roughly $2 to $29 a month depending on the haggle. Offer what the service is worth to you: lower is fine, and Poke will counter if it wants more. This is the one agent where the price is a conversation, not a price list.

What success looks like

You and Poke agree on a monthly price and the subscription is active.

03Connect your first integration

Connect a service you already use: Gmail, Calendar, Notion, Linear, or Oura. Grant access once and Poke can read from it when you ask. Poke also supports Recipes (saved automations) and MCP for custom tools.

What success looks like

You ask about something only the connected integration knows, and it answers with live data instead of guessing.

04Run the test

Text Poke one question only the connected integration can answer. Then read guide 004 to connect your first API in 10 minutes: the same three moves (get access, connect it, test it) work here.

What success looks like

A live answer from your integration, not a guess from memory. You have a working agent.

When something goes wrong

Poke will not accept my price

Counter once, slightly higher. If it still will not accept, ask what it needs: the haggle is part of the product, not a bug.

It answered from memory instead of using the integration

Name the integration explicitly: "Check my [calendar / email / Notion] and read the answer from there." Then ask again. Guide 004, section 05 covers this fix.

Instinct

10 minutesBeginnerFree during beta
What you end up with

An Instinct agent with its own phone number and email, connected to your accounts, ready to act for you. Instinct is invite-only: you join the waitlist or get an invite link from an existing member.

01Get an invite

Two doors. Door one: join the waitlist at app.instinct.com and wait for your invite. Door two: get an invite link from someone who already has Instinct. Each member gets 5 invites to share. If you know a member, door two is faster.

What success looks like

You have an invite link and can sign up.

02Sign up with your phone

Open your invite link and sign in with your phone number plus a text code. You must be 18 or older. There is no app to install: Instinct lives in your messages (iMessage, WhatsApp, SMS) and on calls.

What success looks like

You are signed in and your Instinct agent has its own phone number and email address.

03Connect your accounts

Connect your email, calendar, and messaging apps. This is what turns Instinct from a chatbot into an agent: with your accounts connected, it can book, buy, follow up, and call on your behalf instead of just answering questions.

What success looks like

You ask about something only your connected accounts know, and it answers with live data instead of guessing.

04Hand it one real job

Give it something that needs action, not just an answer: book a table, follow up on an email thread, research and buy a product. Instinct's agents coordinate with each other through a trusted network (Instinct-to-Instinct only), including group chats where your friends do not need accounts.

What success looks like

It does the job and reports back with proof, not just a plan. You have a working agent.

When something goes wrong

I have no invite and the waitlist is slow

Ask around: every member gets 5 invites. One invite link from a member skips the waitlist entirely.

It answered instead of acting

Be explicit about the outcome: "Book the table for two on Friday at 7pm and send me the confirmation." The delegation recipe from guide 001 applies here: outcome, context, constraints, check.
